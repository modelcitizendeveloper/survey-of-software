AI pull-request reviewers compared on trigger, data posture and price shape; most of the field ties on the one open benchmark.

## At a glance


| Library | Best for |
|---|---|
| **CodeRabbit** | Low-effort ambient review where a reader can triage high recall |
| **Greptile** | Teams wanting codebase-graph context and a self-host/BYO-model Enterprise path |
| **Cursor Bugbot** | Cursor users; moderate PR volume |
| **Qodo / PR-Agent** | Top benchmark tier (hosted) or the most portable self-run reviewer (PR-Agent) |
| **GitHub Copilot code review** | GitHub-centric teams on public repos or with ample Actions minutes |
| **Anthropic Code Review / Claude Code /code-review** | Teams already on Claude Code; invoked deep review of risky PRs |
| **Kodus** | Self-hosted, multi-host, precision-leaning |
| **cubic** | Top benchmark tier at per-seat pricing |
| **Augment (Cosmos)** | Teams on Augment's platform who accept risk-based auto-approval |


**What the research found**

- **On Martian's offline benchmark (50 PRs, 98 core issues), Qodo Extended, cubic and Augment are the top three under all three judge models; most of the next fifteen tools have overlapping 95% intervals.** — Benchmark rank separates the top and bottom of the field, not the middle. Vendor '#1' claims each cite a different snapshot.
- **Individual reviewers pass 20-32% of c-CRAB's executable review tests; four combined pass 41.5%.** — Two reviewers from different families are worth more than one better-ranked reviewer.
- **In the one industrial before/after study, AI review comments were 73.8% resolved but PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m.** — Noise has a measurable cost when humans read the comments.
- **GitHub Copilot code review on private repos consumes Actions minutes as well as AI credits since 2026-06-01.** — On a capped, shared Actions pool, an ambient platform reviewer competes with test suites.
- **Flat-seat pricing is insensitive to PR count; metered review is linear. At 300 PRs per seat per month, metered options cost about 12x-300x a $24 flat seat (list prices).** — Agent-authored PR volume makes price shape the dominant cost variable.


**What the research recommends**

**Default** — No category-wide default. Choose the cell (ambient vs invoked × vendor cloud vs your own provider), then the price shape for your PR volume, then precision vs recall for whoever reads the comments. Keep the reviewer advisory.

| Persona | Pick |
|---|---|
| solo maintainer agent fleet | Invoked review by the authoring session, with cross-family or executed verification on risky PRs; optional self-run or flat-seat ambient pass. Avoid metered ambient review, Actions-metered review on a capped pool, and auto-approval. |
| oss maintainer | Free-for-public hosted bot (CodeRabbit, Sourcery, Greptile) configured for precision. |
| product team | Trial two tools on your own repos and score by the team's acceptance rate; prefer tools that cannot approve. |
| regulated enterprise | PR-Agent on internal runners against the approved model endpoint; vendor self-host tiers with BYO model next. |
| non github host | PR-Agent or Kodus; GitLab Duo on GitLab. |

# AI Pull-Request Review: Domain Explainer

## What problem this solves

Code review is the step where someone other than the author reads a change before it
merges. It catches bugs, spreads knowledge and enforces conventions. It is also slow:
reviewers are busy, and a PR waiting for review is work that is done but not shipped.

AI PR reviewers put a language model at that step. They read the diff, sometimes with
the files around it, and post comments the way a human reviewer would. The promise is a
first pass that is always available. The risk is a reviewer that is confidently wrong,
noisy, or silent when it matters.

The question has become more pressing as coding agents write more of the code. A team
whose agents open dozens of PRs a day cannot give each one a careful human read, and a
reviewer from the same model as the author may share the author's blind spots.

## Key concepts

**Diff vs context.** The diff is the lines that changed. Many bugs are visible only with
context: the caller of a changed function, the schema a query assumes, the test that no
longer covers a branch. Tools differ mostly in how much context they fetch and how. More
context is not automatically better; one 2026 benchmark found that adding raw
repository context lowered detection rates.

**Precision and recall.** Precision is the share of a reviewer's comments that point at
a real issue. Recall is the share of real issues it commented on. A reviewer that posts
forty comments will have high recall and low precision; one that posts two will have the
reverse. F1 combines them into one number, at the cost of hiding which way a tool
leans.

**Golden comments.** Benchmarks need ground truth: a list of the issues a PR actually
had. Usually humans write it. Any issue the humans missed counts against a tool that
found it, so benchmark precision understates real precision somewhat.

**LLM judge.** Matching a tool's comment to a golden issue is itself done by a language
model in most benchmarks. A different judge model gives different scores.

**Ambient vs invoked.** An ambient reviewer runs on every PR without being asked. An
invoked reviewer runs when someone, or an agent, asks. Ambient gives coverage without
discipline; invoked gives control without coverage.

**Advisory vs gating.** An advisory reviewer posts comments. A gating reviewer can block
or approve a merge. Some AI reviewers can satisfy a "requires approval" rule on their
own, which changes what that rule means.

**Self-preference.** Language models rate their own outputs higher than others' outputs
of similar quality. When the same model family writes and reviews code, the reviewer
may be more likely to accept the author's mistakes.

**Verification pass.** A second stage that tries to confirm each candidate finding
before posting it, by re-reading the code, running a test, or reproducing the bug. It
trades latency and cost for fewer false alarms.

## The shape of the market

Three kinds of product:

1. **Hosted bots** you install as a code-host app (CodeRabbit, Greptile, Cursor Bugbot,
   Qodo and others). The vendor's servers read your code.
2. **Platform-native reviewers** inside a code host or suite you already pay for
   (GitHub Copilot, GitLab Duo, Gemini Code Assist enterprise, Amazon Q).
3. **Self-run reviewers** you install in your own CI or server with your own model key
   (PR-Agent, Kodus, and coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode packaged
   for CI).

Pricing comes in two shapes: a flat monthly fee per developer, or a charge per review,
per line or per credit. The second shape grows with the number of PRs, which agent-
written code tends to increase sharply.

## Common misconceptions

**"The benchmark leader is the best reviewer."** On the one open, independent
benchmark, the top few tools are separable from the bottom few, but most of the middle
cannot be told apart on 50 PRs. Vendors each cite a different snapshot in which they
are first.

**"An AI reviewer makes review faster."** The one industrial before/after study found
PRs took longer to close after an AI reviewer was added, because developers spent time
on its comments.

**"One good reviewer is enough."** Reviewers miss most issues and miss different ones.
Four reviewers combined caught about ten points more of a benchmark's issues than the
best one alone.

**"It's free because it runs in our CI."** Self-run reviewers cost model tokens and CI
minutes. Since June 2026, GitHub's own Copilot reviewer also consumes Actions minutes on
private repositories.

## How to choose

1. Decide whether a new party may read your code. That splits the market in half.
2. Decide who will read the comments: humans (favor precision) or agents (can absorb
   recall).
3. Estimate PRs per developer per month. That picks the price shape.
4. Keep the reviewer advisory, keep its rules in the repository, and watch for it going
   quiet.

# S1 Rapid Discovery — Approach

## What this category is

**AI pull-request review tools** read a proposed change (a PR or merge request diff,
usually with some surrounding repository context) and post findings back: inline
comments, a summary, sometimes a suggested fix. They sit at the merge gate, between
"the author thinks it is done" and "it lands on main."

The test for inclusion: **the tool's primary output is review findings on a diff**, and
it can attach those findings to a PR on a code host (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure
DevOps, Gitea/Forgejo), whether it runs on its own or when someone asks.

## Category boundary

**In scope**

- Hosted review bots installed as a code-host app: CodeRabbit, Greptile, Graphite,
  Cursor Bugbot, Qodo, Sourcery, cubic, CodeAnt, Bito, Baz, Kodus Cloud, Augment.
- Review built into the code host or a platform suite: GitHub Copilot code review,
  GitLab Duo Code Review, Gemini Code Assist (enterprise), Amazon Q Developer.
- Self-run reviewers you install in your own CI or on your own server: PR-Agent,
  Kodus (self-hosted), claude-code-action, openai/codex-action, run-gemini-cli,
  the OpenCode GitHub agent.
- Invoked review from a coding agent: Claude Code `/code-review` and its cloud
  `ultra` mode, Codex `@codex review`, CodeRabbit CLI, plus Anthropic's managed Code
  Review service.

**Out of scope**

- **Deterministic static analysis** (linters, type checkers, SAST rule engines). These
  are rule-based and covered in 1.250 Linters & Static Analysis and 1.251 Type Checkers.
  Several AI reviewers run linters internally and post the results; that does not move
  linters into this category.
- **Coding agent harnesses** as such (1.212). Claude Code and Codex appear here only in
  their review role.
- **Human review workflow tools** (stacked-diff UIs, review queues, merge queues)
  except where the vendor's AI reviewer is the product being compared.
- **AI that applies fixes to human review comments** (the reverse direction; e.g. Meta's
  2025 work on auto-fixing reviewer comments).

## Evaluation dimensions

Each option is profiled on the same six dimensions:

1. **Trigger model** — ambient (reviews every PR on open/push), summoned (a comment
   command), or invoked (someone runs it from a terminal or a CI job they wrote).
2. **Where it runs and what it consumes** — vendor cloud, your CI runners (and their
   minutes), your own server, or your workstation.
3. **Pricing shape** — flat per seat, metered per review / per line / per credit, or
   your own model tokens plus compute.
4. **Data posture** — retention, training on customer code, self-host or VPC option,
   bring-your-own model key.
5. **Output and control** — inline comments, severity, suggested fixes, whether it can
   approve or block, config files and learned rules.
6. **Evidence of quality** — who measured it, on what, with what sample size.

## How volatile this is

High. In the twelve months to October 2026: Cursor agreed to acquire Graphite
(source: https://cursor.com/blog/graphite, accessed 2026-10-02); Qodo handed PR-Agent
to a community org (source: https://www.qodo.ai/blog/qodo-is-handing-pr-agent-over-to-the-community/,
accessed 2026-10-02); Google shut down the free consumer Gemini Code Assist GitHub app
(source: https://developers.google.com/gemini-code-assist/docs/deprecations/consumer-code-review,
accessed 2026-10-02); Cursor Bugbot, Greptile and GitHub Copilot all moved toward
metered billing; Augment rebuilt its reviewer on a new agent platform and retired
individual plans. `decay_class: fast`.

Sources for every figure in this pass are inline. Vendor statements are marked
`(vendor: ...)`; figures in circulation that could not be traced to a primary page are
marked `(unverified)`.

# Invoked review from a coding agent

A developer, or an agent session, runs the review on purpose. Nothing watches the
repository. The review lands when someone asks for it, which is a weakness for a team
that forgets and a strength for a team that wants every review to be a decision.

## Claude Code `/code-review`

- Runs locally as a background subagent in a Claude Code session, against normal plan
  usage. Targets: the working diff, a PR number, a branch or a path. Effort levels
  `low` to `max`; low and medium return fewer, high-confidence findings, high and above
  return broader, less certain ones. `--comment` posts inline to GitHub or as a GitLab
  MR note; `--fix` applies findings
  (vendor: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review, accessed 2026-10-02).
- **`/code-review ultra`** (also `claude ultrareview` for CI) sends the branch to a cloud
  fleet of agents in which every finding is independently reproduced and verified before
  it is reported. About 5-10 minutes; Pro and Max get three free runs, then about $5-25
  per review in usage credits; default limit 500 files / 8,000 lines; research preview
  (vendor: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/ultrareview, accessed 2026-10-02).

## Anthropic Code Review (managed)

The ambient sibling of the above, run on Anthropic's infrastructure for Team and
Enterprise: on PR open, on every push, or by `@claude review`. Parallel agents each hunt
one class of issue, then a verification step checks candidates against the code. It
posts a neutral check run and **never approves or blocks**. Average about 20 minutes and
$15-25 per review; not available to Zero Data Retention orgs; configured through
`CLAUDE.md` and `REVIEW.md`
(vendor: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review, accessed 2026-10-02).
Anthropic's launch figures: under 1% of findings marked incorrect by its engineers;
84% of PRs over 1,000 lines received findings, averaging 7.5 issues
(vendor, reported at https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/10/anthropic-claude-code-review/).

## Codex

`@codex review` in the PR (see platform-native) or a `/review` against a base branch in
the Codex CLI (unverified on primary docs).

## CodeRabbit CLI

Proprietary local CLI that reviews uncommitted or branch changes before a PR exists.
Free tier with daily limits; a usage add-on is billed per file; `--agent` emits NDJSON
for a coding agent to consume (vendor: https://docs.coderabbit.ai/cli/, accessed
2026-10-02).

## The self-review question

An invoked reviewer from the same agent that wrote the code is the cheapest path for a
team already using that agent: no new vendor, no new data recipient, no CI minutes. It
is also a reviewer from the same model family as the author. Evidence on whether that
matters is in S2.

# Review built into the code host or a platform suite

These ship inside something the team already pays for. The appeal is no new vendor;
the cost is that pricing, data handling and roadmap follow the platform, not the
reviewer.

## GitHub Copilot code review

- **Trigger:** request Copilot as a reviewer, or enable automatic review on PR open
  (optionally on each push). It reviews once by default
  (source: https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/code-review, accessed 2026-10-02).
- **Where it runs:** on GitHub Actions. **From 2026-06-01, reviews of private
  repositories consume the account's Actions minutes** (overage at standard Actions
  rates) in addition to Copilot AI credits. Public repos are unaffected; self-hosted and
  larger runners are supported and billed at their own rates
  (source: https://github.blog/changelog/2026-04-27-github-copilot-code-review-will-start-consuming-github-actions-minutes-on-june-1-2026/,
  accessed 2026-10-02).
- **Per-review cost is unsettled in GitHub's own docs.** The billing page fetched on
  2026-10-02 still described 13 premium requests per review; the changelog says premium
  requests were replaced by token-based AI credits. Treat the per-review figure as
  unconfirmed.
- **Output:** an overview with an approval assessment, inline comments. It can submit an
  approving review that satisfies required-approval rules when an admin enables that;
  new commits dismiss the approval.
- **Config:** `.github/copilot-instructions.md`. No model choice.

Copilot is the only reviewer in this survey that draws on two meters at once: model
credits and CI minutes. For an organization whose Actions minutes are a shared,
capped pool, that second meter is the deciding fact.

## GitLab Duo Code Review

GitLab's built-in reviewer for merge requests. It appears on Martian's offline
benchmark (see S2). Pricing and data terms were not checked for this pass
(unverified).

## Gemini Code Assist

The **free consumer GitHub app is gone**: deprecated 2026-06-18 and shut down
2026-07-17, with all review activity ended
(source: https://developers.google.com/gemini-code-assist/docs/deprecations/consumer-code-review,
accessed 2026-10-02). The enterprise edition, tied to Google Cloud, continues; its review
pricing was not found (unverified). Teams that adopted the free app in 2025 lost their
reviewer with a month's notice.

## Amazon Q Developer

GitHub PR review (auto on open, `/q` comments, generated fixes) is still labeled
**Preview**, and Q Developer IDE plugin support ends 2027-04-30 as AWS moves users to
Kiro (source: https://aws.amazon.com/q/developer/, accessed 2026-10-02). Whether PR
review continues inside Kiro is not stated (unverified).

## OpenAI Codex review

`@codex review` on a PR, or automatic review enabled per repository; rules go in an
`AGENTS.md` "Code Review Rules" section; `@codex fix it` follows up with a change
(source: https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/third-party/github, accessed 2026-10-02). It
runs on OpenAI's cloud and is included in ChatGPT plan usage rather than priced per
review. The same model can also be run in your own CI through `openai/codex-action`
(see self-run).

# S1 Recommendation — the map

## Two axes decide most of the choice

The field sorts on **who pulls the trigger** and **where the code goes**.

| | **Vendor cloud** (new party reads the code) | **Your CI / your server** (only your model provider reads it) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ambient** (every PR, unprompted) | CodeRabbit, Greptile, Graphite, Bugbot, cubic, Qodo, Sourcery, Bito, Baz, Augment, Anthropic Code Review, Copilot\* | PR-Agent, Kodus self-hosted, claude-code-action / codex-action / run-gemini-cli / OpenCode wired to `pull_request` events |
| **Invoked** (someone asks) | `/code-review ultra`, `@codex review`, comment-summoned modes of the bots above | Claude Code `/code-review` local, CodeRabbit CLI, PR-Agent CLI, `kodus review` |

\*Copilot runs in the vendor's cloud for the model and on **your** Actions minutes for
the job, so it sits in both columns for billing purposes.

Pick the cell first. A team that cannot add a data recipient is choosing from the right
column whatever the benchmark says. A team that wants every PR covered without anyone
remembering is choosing from the top row.

## Price shape is the third axis

Flat seat (CodeRabbit, Graphite, Sourcery, Bito, Kodus) versus metered (Greptile,
Bugbot, cubic, Qodo, Baz, Augment, Anthropic Code Review, Copilot). Per-review costs on
the metered side run from about $1 (Bugbot, vendor) to $15-25 (Anthropic Code Review,
vendor). A team's PR count, not its headcount, decides which shape is cheaper. Teams
whose PRs are mostly written by agents have high PR counts per human seat, which favors
flat seats for ambient review.

## What the field looks like in October 2026

- **No stable leader on quality.** On the one independent, open benchmark, the top three
  are separable from the bottom few but the middle fifteen are not (S2).
- **Consolidation and exits.** Graphite to Cursor; PR-Agent to a community org; Gemini's
  free app shut down; Korbit reported shut down (unverified date); Ellipsis's pricing
  page now sells general cloud coding agents (source: https://www.ellipsis.dev/pricing,
  accessed 2026-10-02) rather than a review bot.
- **Metering is spreading.** Three major products moved from seats to usage in 2026.

## Carried into S2

CodeRabbit, Greptile, Cursor Bugbot, Qodo / PR-Agent, Copilot code review, Anthropic Code
Review and Claude Code `/code-review`, Kodus, cubic, Augment. The rest stay in the S1
tables.

## PR-Agent

The longest-running open-source reviewer (`/review`, `/describe`, `/improve`, `/ask`
comment commands). Qodo built it, let it go quiet in late 2025 while it focused on its
hosted product, and on 2026-04-23 handed it to a community organization with a
three-person governance committee that includes an outside maintainer

# S2 Comprehensive — Approach

Three questions decide whether an AI reviewer is worth its cost, and each has a
different kind of evidence:

1. **Does it find real problems, and how much noise comes with them?** Benchmarks
   (precision, recall, F1 against a labeled set) and field studies (what share of
   comments developers acted on). Covered in `benchmark-evidence.md` and
   `field-evidence-and-self-review.md`.
2. **What does it cost at a given PR volume?** List prices converted to a per-month bill
   at three volumes. `cost-at-volume.md`.
3. **What does it do mechanically?** Trigger, re-review on push, where it runs, what it
   can block, how it is configured. `feature-comparison.md`.

# Benchmark evidence

## Martian Code Review Bench

Martian is a research lab that does not sell a reviewer. Its benchmark is open (MIT):
data, judge prompts and pipeline are published at
https://github.com/withmartian/code-review-benchmark (accessed 2026-10-02).

- **Offline set:** 50 PRs from five open-source repositories (Cal.com, Discourse,
  Grafana, Keycloak, Sentry), with human-curated "golden" comments. 98 golden issues in
  the default `core` profile.
- **Judging:** an LLM judge matches each tool's comments to golden issues. Three judges
  are published: Claude Opus 4.5 (default), Claude Sonnet 4.5 and GPT-5.2. Two of the
  judge vendors also have products on the board.
- **Online set:** about 300,000 real PRs, scored by whether developers acted on the bot's
  comments (source: methodology/full.md in the repository).
- **Stated limits** (Martian's own): static dataset with contamination risk, a golden set
  that caps recall, judge calibration without formal human validation, and it scores
  products, not models.

- **The judge moves the numbers.** Most tools score lower under Sonnet 4.5 and GPT-5.2

  this snapshot is near-silent: every comment it made matched, but it found 7.6% of the
  issues.
- **A low-precision reviewer costs reader time on every PR;** a low-recall one costs
  missed bugs on some PRs. Which costs more depends on who reads the comments. An agent
  reading them is cheaper per comment than a human.

## c-CRAB (academic, executable)

NUS, Zhejiang University and SonarSource, arXiv 2603.23448 (2026-03-30): 184 PR instances
across 67 repositories, each with executable tests that pass only if the review
identified the problem the test encodes. Claude Code, Codex, Devin Review and PR-Agent individually
passed 20.1%-32.1% of the 234 tests; all four combined reached 41.5%. Manual analysis
judged 84% of generated comments useful even where the test failed
(source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23448v2, accessed 2026-10-02).

Two readings: individual reviewers miss most issues a human reviewer catches, and
**different reviewers miss different issues** — the union beats any member by about ten
points.

## SWE-PRBench

arXiv 2603.26130 (March 2026): 350 human-annotated PRs across 65 repositories; eight
frontier models detected 15-31% of human-flagged issues from the diff, and adding more
raw repository context lowered performance (unverified; read from the abstract summary
only).

## Vendor-run benchmarks

- **Greptile, July 2025:** 50 PRs, catch rate Greptile 82%, Bugbot 58%, Copilot 54%,
  CodeRabbit 44%, Graphite 6% (vendor: Greptile; read via a secondary summary,
  unverified against the primary).
- **Entelligence, September 2026:** 67 real bugs across five repositories, eight tools;
  Entelligence ranks itself first at F1 47.2 (vendor: https://entelligence.ai/code-review-benchmark-2026).
- **Bugbot:** "80% resolution rate" at default effort (vendor: Cursor docs).
- **Augment:** 65% precision on its own codebase (vendor).

A vendor's own benchmark ranks the vendor first in every case found. They are useful
as descriptions of what each vendor optimizes for, not as rankings.

# Configuration examples

## A self-run ambient reviewer: PR-Agent as a GitHub Action

From PR-Agent's installation docs (source:
https://github.com/The-PR-Agent/pr-agent/blob/main/docs/docs/installation/github.md,
read 2026-10-02):

```yaml
# .github/workflows/pr_agent.yml
on:
  pull_request:
    types: [opened, reopened, ready_for_review, synchronize]
  issue_comment:
jobs:
  pr_agent_job:
    if: ${{ github.event.sender.type != 'Bot' }}
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    permissions:
      issues: write
      pull-requests: write
      contents: write
      checks: write
    name: Run pr agent on every pull request, respond to user comments
    steps:
      - name: PR Agent action step
        id: pragent
        uses: the-pr-agent/pr-agent@main
        env:
          OPENAI_KEY: ${{ secrets.OPENAI_KEY }}
          GITHUB_TOKEN: ${{ secrets.GITHUB_TOKEN }}
```

Three changes a cost- or security-conscious team usually makes:

- **`runs-on: self-hosted`** moves the job off the hosted minute pool onto your own
  runner. The model call is the only thing that leaves.
- **Drop `synchronize`** from `types` to review once per PR rather than on every push.
  Comment commands (`/review`) still re-run it on demand.
- **Pin the action** to a release tag or commit instead of `@main`.

Repository-level settings go in `.pr_agent.toml` on the **default branch**. PR-Agent's
docs warn against pointing the config branch at PR-controlled input: anyone who can push
a branch could then supply a config that redirects the model endpoint, injects
instructions, or enables auto-approval of their own PR (source:
https://github.com/The-PR-Agent/pr-agent/blob/main/docs/docs/usage-guide/configuration_options.md,
read 2026-10-02). The same class of risk applies to any reviewer that reads its rules
from the branch under review.

## A liveness check for an ambient reviewer

An ambient bot that stops posting looks like one that found nothing. This script lists
recent PRs and the minutes until a given bot first commented or reviewed, using the
GitHub CLI's REST access. Set `BOT` to the reviewer's login.

```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# usage: review-latency.sh owner/repo bot-login [count]
set -euo pipefail
REPO=$1; BOT=$2; N=${3:-10}
gh pr list -R "$REPO" --state all --limit "$N" --json number,createdAt \
  --jq '.[] | "\(.number) \(.createdAt)"' |
while read -r num created; do
  first=$( { gh api "repos/$REPO/pulls/$num/reviews" --paginate \
               --jq ".[] | select(.user.login==\"$BOT\") | .submitted_at";
             gh api "repos/$REPO/issues/$num/comments" --paginate \
               --jq ".[] | select(.user.login==\"$BOT\") | .created_at"; } | sort | head -1)
  if [ -z "$first" ]; then
    echo "#$num  no review from $BOT"
  else
    mins=$(( ( $(date -d "$first" +%s) - $(date -d "$created" +%s) ) / 60 ))
    echo "#$num  first review after ${mins} min"
  fi
done
```

A switch trigger can then be written as a rule over this output, such as "no review
within 30 minutes on three consecutive PRs." The threshold is the team's choice.

## Mechanics that change the choice

- **Approval authority.** Copilot (when enabled) and Augment's Cosmos reviewer can
  approve a PR. Anthropic's managed reviewer is designed never to. A team whose merge
  rule is "a human or a deliberate gate approves" should check this before installing,
  because an approving bot satisfies required-approval rules.
- **Re-review on push** is what makes ambient review expensive under metering: a PR
  with eight fix-up pushes is nine reviews. Bugbot and Anthropic expose the setting; on
  others it is implicit.
- **Config lives in the repository** for every option here. Moving between reviewers
  means translating one rules file into another, which is cheap; learned rules that live
  in the vendor's database (Bugbot `@cursor remember`, CodeRabbit learnings) do not move.
- **Model choice** is a self-run property. Among hosted bots only Greptile and Qodo
  (Enterprise) and Kodus (all tiers) let you bring the model, which is also how a team
  gets a reviewer from a different family than its author.

# Field evidence, self-review, and verification

## What developers do with AI review comments

| Study | Setting | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cihan et al., ICSE-SEIP 2025 | Industrial deployment of a PR-Agent-based reviewer; 4,335 PRs in three projects, 1,568 reviewed by the tool | 73.8% of automated comments resolved; **PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m**; complaints: faulty reviews, unnecessary corrections, irrelevant comments | https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18531 |
| Cynthia, Widyasari, Roy, Zhang, Lo, 2026 | 54,713 agent review comments in 341 Python GitHub repos | Resolved: Copilot 72.9% (n=45,668), Cursor 67.2% (n=6,778), Codex 54.8% (n=2,267); inline suggestion blocks raise adoption (OR 1.62); unresolved mostly intentional design or incorrect suggestion | https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21997v1 |

"Resolved" is not precision. A resolved comment can be a nit fixed to clear the thread.
The three measures in this survey (resolved 55-74%, Martian offline precision about 30-70% for most tools,
c-CRAB executable pass 20-32%) measure different things and should not be compared to
one another.

In the one industrial deployment with a before/after measurement, AI review made PRs
slower to close, not faster.

## Same-model-family review

A reviewer from the same model family as the author is the default when a team uses
one coding agent for both jobs. The evidence:

- **General LLM self-preference is established.** Panickssery, Bowman and Feng
  (arXiv 2404.13076, 2024) showed GPT-4 and Llama 2 recognize their own outputs and
  rate them higher, with preference strength tracking recognition. Wataoka et al.
  (arXiv 2410.21819) attribute the effect to perplexity: judges favor text that is
  familiar to them.
- **Code-specific evidence exists and points the same way.** "Articulate but Wrong"
  (arXiv 2605.21537): across 1,980 code-modernization attempts by 11 models, 31.7% of
  semantically drifted outputs were endorsed by the same model that produced them; the
  self-review miss rate ranged from 0% to 100% depending on the model.
- **No controlled study** was found of "model X writes the PR, model X reviews it" on
  real PR review precision and recall. The case against same-family review is inferred
  from the two lines above, not measured on the task.

Two mitigations appear in practice: a reviewer from a different model family (most
self-run reviewers and several hosted bots let you pick), and a verification step that
must reproduce a finding against the code rather than argue for it.

## Verification passes

Anthropic's managed Code Review and its `ultra` mode, and Augment's Cosmos reviewer,
describe a second stage that tries to confirm or refute each candidate finding before
posting (vendor). No independent measurement of that stage on PR review was found.

Adjacent evidence, all from vulnerability detection rather than PR review:

- A heterogeneous adversarial verifier (a small local Qwen3-8B checking DeepSeek-V3
  experts) raised precision by 10.3 points on 262 NIST Juliet samples (arXiv 2604.21282;
  synthetic benchmark). The author chose a different model family for the verifier.
- An LLM filter over SAST findings cut false positives from 560 to 64 with a 3.1% recall
  loss (arXiv 2605.01885; unverified, abstract only).

The pattern these support: **verification that executes something (a test, a mutant, a
reproduction) is stronger evidence than verification that re-reads the diff**, and a
verifier from another model family sidesteps the self-preference problem.

# S2 Recommendation

## Five findings that hold under scrutiny

1. **Quality differences in the middle of the field are smaller than the noise in the
   best available benchmark.** On Martian's 50-PR offline set, three tools (Qodo
   Extended, cubic, Augment) lead under every judge; most of the next fifteen have
   overlapping 95% intervals. Choosing among them on benchmark rank is choosing on noise.
   Choose on trigger, data posture, price shape and precision/recall lean.
2. **Every reviewer misses most issues, and they miss different ones.** c-CRAB: 20-32%
   individually, 41.5% for four combined. Two different reviewers are worth more than
   one better-ranked one, if the budget allows a second.
3. **Precision vs recall is a product choice to match to the reader.** High-recall,
   low-precision bots (CodeRabbit, Copilot) suit a reader who can triage cheaply, such
   as an agent that is told to fix or dismiss each comment. High-precision, lower-recall
   tools (Devin, Baz, Kodus on this benchmark) suit a human reader whose attention is
   the scarce resource.
4. **Price shape dominates price level once PR volume is high.** At 300 PRs per seat
   per month, metered ambient review costs 12x-300x a $24 seat (vendor: list prices in S1)
   (see `cost-at-volume.md`).
5. **Same-family self-review is a known weakness without a measured size.** Use a
   different model family for the reviewer, or a verification step that executes
   something, when the author is an AI agent.

## Engineering stances

- **Ambient, flat, hosted** — CodeRabbit or Sourcery, tuned for precision through the
  config file. Lowest effort; adds a data recipient.
- **Ambient, self-run** — PR-Agent or Kodus with a key from a different model family than
  the author. Keeps code with the existing model provider; costs setup and either CI
  minutes or a small server.
- **Invoked, deep** — a multi-agent verified review (`/code-review ultra`, Anthropic
  Code Review on manual trigger) on the PRs that touch money, auth, migrations or user
  data; nothing or a cheap pass on the rest.
- **Layered** — a cheap ambient pass on every PR plus an invoked deep pass on risky ones.
  The c-CRAB union result is the argument for it.

  cheaply and can take a high-recall reviewer; humans cannot.
- **A second reviewer from a different family beats a better-ranked single one** when
  the miss rate matters (c-CRAB: 41.5% combined vs 32.1% best single).

# Persona: GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure DevOps or Gitea shop

## Situation

The code host is not GitHub, or there are several. Many products in this category
started as GitHub apps.

## What it eliminates

- GitHub-only products: Copilot code review, Anthropic's managed Code Review, the
  GitHub Actions-packaged agents (claude-code-action, codex-action, run-gemini-cli,

On GitLab, Duo Code Review is the no-new-vendor option and sits mid-table on Martian's

# Persona: open-source maintainer

## Situation

Public repositories, outside contributors of uneven experience, no budget, and limited
time to triage. Fork PRs are common.

## What it eliminates

- Anything paid. Several vendors waive fees for public repos, so this is less limiting
  than it sounds.
- **Gemini Code Assist's free GitHub app**, which was the common free choice in 2025 and
  was shut down on 2026-07-17.
- Self-run Actions reviewers on fork PRs, unless the maintainer understands the secret-
  exposure rules for `pull_request_target`. A workflow that hands a model key to code
  from a fork is a security decision, not a convenience.

## What remains

- **CodeRabbit** (public repos free, per vendor), **Sourcery** (public repos free),
  **Greptile** (free for MIT/Apache-licensed projects), **CodeAnt** (free for open
  source on request).
- **Copilot code review** on public repositories is outside the 2026 Actions-minute
  change.

## Recommendation

A free-for-public hosted bot, configured toward precision so contributors are not
buried in comments. Precision matters more here than in a team setting: an outside
contributor who receives fifteen low-value comments on a first PR may not return.
CodeRabbit leans high-recall on Martian's benchmark (precision 32.6%, `core` profile);
its path filters and instructions are the lever to pull.

# Persona: product team, 5-50 engineers

## Situation

Private repositories, mostly human-written PRs, a human reviewer already required on
every PR. The AI reviewer is a first pass that should catch what tired humans miss and
stay quiet otherwise. Seat budget exists; a new SaaS vendor is acceptable after a
security review.

## What it eliminates

- High-noise configurations. A human reads every comment; a low-precision bot taxes
  every PR and gets ignored. The one industrial before/after study found AI review
  lengthened PR closure time (5h52m to 8h20m; Cihan et al., ICSE-SEIP 2025).
- Vendors without a published data policy, for teams whose security review requires one.

## What remains

- **Flat-seat:** CodeRabbit, Graphite, Sourcery, Bito.
- **Metered at moderate volume (20-50 PRs per engineer per month):** Greptile basic,
  Bugbot, cubic, Qodo. At this volume they cost about the same as a seat.
- **Top of Martian's offline set:** Qodo Extended, cubic, Augment.

## Recommendation

Trial two tools for two weeks each on the same repositories and count the comments the
team acted on. Benchmark rank in the middle of the field does not separate products;
the team's own acceptance rate does. Prefer a tool that cannot approve, so the human
review requirement stays meaningful.

# Persona: regulated enterprise

## Situation

Source code may not leave an approved boundary, or may go only to an approved model
provider under an existing agreement. Audit trails are required. Changes need a
recorded human approval.

## What it eliminates

- Hosted bots without a self-host, VPC or single-tenant option.
- Any reviewer that can satisfy a required-approval rule on its own.
- Anthropic's managed Code Review for orgs on Zero Data Retention (it is not offered
  to them, vendor docs).

## What remains

- **Self-run:** PR-Agent (MIT, any model via LiteLLM including Bedrock, Vertex and
  local models), Kodus self-hosted (AGPLv3; check the license against internal policy),
  claude-code-action or codex-action against the provider already under contract.
- **Vendor self-host tiers:** CodeRabbit Enterprise, Greptile Enterprise (BYO model),
  Sourcery Enterprise (BYO model), Qodo Enterprise (on-prem), Bito Professional.

## Recommendation

PR-Agent on internal runners against the already-approved model endpoint is the
shortest path through procurement: no new vendor, permissive license, a governance
committee since April 2026. If the review quality bar is higher than a single model
call, a vendor self-host tier is the next step. Check whether its multi-agent features
survive self-hosting.

# Persona: solo maintainer running a fleet of coding agents

## Situation

One human owns several private, IP-sensitive repositories. Most PRs are written by
AI coding agents the maintainer supervises. PR volume per human is high (hundreds a
month across repositories is plausible) and PRs arrive in bursts. CI runs on a hosted
plan whose minutes are one pool across all repositories with a hard spend stop. The
maintainer wants findings surfaced at the merge gate, not automatic approval.

## What it eliminates

- **Metered per-review hosted bots for ambient coverage.** At high PR volume the
  arithmetic in S2's cost table goes against Greptile advanced reviews, Bugbot, and
  Anthropic's managed review on every push. Re-review on each fix-up push multiplies it.
- **Copilot code review on private repos.** It draws on Actions minutes as well as model
  credits since 2026-06-01, so it competes with the test suites for the capped pool.
- **Any reviewer that can approve.** Copilot's approval mode and Augment's risk-based
  auto-approval would let an agent-authored PR pass the gate with no deliberate review.
- **Hosted bots that add a data recipient**, unless the maintainer accepts that
  disclosure for the repositories involved.

## What remains

1. **Invoked review run by the agent session that wrote the diff**, before it calls the
   PR done. Zero marginal price inside an existing coding-agent subscription; no new
   recipient; no CI minutes. The weakness is same-family self-review. Mitigate with:
   - a higher effort level or a multi-agent mode whose findings must be reproduced, and
   - review prompts that make the reviewer run code (tests, mutants) rather than read it.
2. **A self-run ambient pass with a different model family** (PR-Agent or Kodus, on a
   self-hosted runner or a small server to keep it off the shared minute pool). Cheap
   per PR, catches a different set of issues (c-CRAB's union result).
3. **A flat-seat hosted bot** (CodeRabbit, Sourcery) if a new data recipient is
   acceptable. Flat pricing absorbs agent PR volume.

## Watch for

- **Width.** Multi-agent review modes list at about $5-25 per review against
  about $1.00-1.50 for a single-pass hosted bot (vendor list prices, S1). Run as an
  everyday gate, a fan-out reviewer can consume a usage window quickly. Price the
  number of agents before launching, and keep the wide mode for risky diffs.
- **Bot silence.** An ambient bot that stops posting looks the same as an ambient bot
  that found nothing. A gate that depends on an ambient bot needs a liveness check.

## Recommendation

Invoked review by default, with a cross-family or executed-verification step on PRs
touching auth, money, migrations or user data. Add an ambient pass only if PRs are being
merged without anyone invoking review, and then prefer self-run or flat-seat.

# S4 Strategic — Viability signals

A reviewer is a cheap thing to switch in principle (its config is a file in the
repository) and an expensive thing to lose in practice (a merge gate that silently stops
running). The viability signals that matter here:

1. **Exit history in the last year.** Has the product, or a sibling product from the
   same vendor, been shut down, sold or handed off?
2. **Pricing-model churn.** A move from flat to metered changes the bill at the next
   renewal without any change in usage.
3. **Platform dependency.** A reviewer bundled with a larger platform follows that
   platform's strategy (Gemini's consumer app, Amazon Q's move to Kiro).
4. **Governance of open-source options.** Who can merge, and whether the project
   survived a sponsor stepping back.
5. **Lock-in surface.** Rules in a repo file move; learned rules in a vendor database
   and approval integrations do not.

Funding is used below only as a durability fact, never as a quality signal.


---

# S4 Recommendation

**Optimize for replaceability.** No reviewer in this category has a durable quality lead
that a benchmark can show, and the field lost or reshaped several products in a year.
The posture that survives:

1. **Keep review rules in the repository** in a form that can be translated (a
   `REVIEW.md`, `AGENTS.md` section, or `.coderabbit.yaml`), not only in vendor-side
   learned rules.
2. **Make the reviewer advisory, not the approver.** The merge decision stays with a
   human or a deliberate gate; the reviewer's absence then degrades coverage instead of
   blocking merges.
3. **Watch for silence.** An ambient reviewer that stopped posting is indistinguishable
   from one that found nothing. Track time-from-PR-open-to-first-review and alert when
   it exceeds a threshold over several consecutive PRs.
4. **Set a switch trigger before you need it.** Examples: review latency over a stated
   number of minutes on N consecutive PRs; a renewal that changes price shape; monthly
   spend crossing a stated multiple of the plan's list price; a vendor announcement that
   moves the reviewer under a different product.
5. **Prefer a reviewer whose model you can change** where the author is also an AI. A
   self-run or BYO-model reviewer lets you keep the reviewer's family different from
   the author's as both evolve.

## By strategic path

- **Conservative:** self-run PR-Agent or Kodus with a model key you already hold,
  advisory only.
- **Low-effort:** flat-seat hosted bot, advisory, with a liveness check.
- **Depth-first:** invoked multi-agent verified review on risky PRs, plus a cheap
  ambient pass everywhere.
