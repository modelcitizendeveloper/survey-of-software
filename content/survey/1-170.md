# Machine Translation APIs for CJK: Domain Explainer

## What This Solves

**The problem:** An application needs to translate content between Chinese, Japanese, Korean and other languages automatically, at scale, with acceptable quality.

**Who encounters this:** Product managers launching in Asian markets, engineering teams building international features, content teams localizing for Chinese, Japanese and Korean (CJK) audiences.

**Why it matters:** Human translation is slow and priced per word. Machine translation APIs are priced per character, in the range of $10-25 per million characters at January 2026 list prices (Amazon's $15 confirmed 2026-11-05), and respond instantly. That enables use cases human translation cannot support, such as real-time chat translation and million-product catalogs.

## Accessible Analogies

### Translation as a Service
Think of a translation API like electricity: you do not build the power plant, you plug into the grid and pay for what you use. The grid is a hosted neural network trained on very large text collections. You send text to a URL, receive the translation, and are billed by characters.

### Quality as a Spectrum
Machine translation is not right or wrong; it ranges from gist-level to publication-ready.
- **Gist:** understand a message (chat, reviews)
- **Working draft:** a human edits it (documentation, marketing)
- **Final:** reviewed by a native speaker (contracts, public product copy)

### CJK Challenges
- **No spaces between words** in Chinese and Japanese, so the system must infer word boundaries.
- **Two Chinese scripts**, Simplified and Traditional, with regional vocabulary differences.
- **Japanese mixes three scripts** and has politeness levels built into verb forms.
- **Korean has speech levels** that depend on the relationship between speaker and listener.

### Formality as a Dimension
Japanese politeness (keigo) is like a dress code: casual for friends, polite for business, honorific for a company president. Sending casual Japanese to a business partner is like arriving at a board meeting in flip-flops. Amazon documents a formality setting for Japanese and Korean targets (AWS documentation, November 2026), and DeepL documents one for Japanese. Azure added tone control on its LLM path in 2026. No formality control was found for Google or the Azure neural machine translation (NMT) path. No Chinese register control was found at any of the four.

## When You Need This

**Clear yes:** content volume beyond what human translators can handle; real-time needs; user-generated content; a first draft for human review.

**Clear no:** legal or medical text without review; brand-critical copy; content where an error carries liability.

**Gray area:** documentation and marketing, where machine output plus human review often works.

## Trade-offs

### Quality and Cost
Dedicated translation APIs sit between free consumer tools and human translators: lower cost than human work, with quality below skilled human translators. Quality varies by language pair and domain, so test with your own text.

### Build or Buy
**Use an API when** volume is moderate to high, speed matters, and no domain-specific model is required.

**Self-hosted open-source models** (for example Opus-MT or No Language Left Behind) suit teams with machine learning expertise, strict data residency needs, or very high volume. They need infrastructure and maintenance, and their quality varies by pair.

### Cloud Services
Google, Azure and Amazon translation services integrate with their clouds (storage, identity, monitoring) and couple your pipeline to that cloud. DeepL is standalone and easier to move.

### LLMs as Translators
Google and Azure now offer LLM-based translation next to NMT, and DeepL describes a next-generation model. A general-purpose chat-model API is another route, priced per token. Check determinism, data terms and cost on long documents.

## Cost Considerations

### Pricing Models
All four bill per character of source text. Prices per million characters (January 2026 list prices, unconfirmed since, except Amazon confirmed 2026-11-05):

| Provider | Standard text | Free allowance |
|---|---|---|
| Azure | $10 | 2M a month (confirmed 2026-11-05) |
| Amazon | $15 | 2M a month for 12 months (confirmed 2026-11-05) |
| Google | $20 | 500K a month |
| DeepL | $25 plus $5.49 a month | 500K a month; current plans unconfirmed |

### Hidden Costs
Re-translating edited content, document minimums (DeepL bills at least 50,000 characters per document-format file such as Word or PDF), custom model hosting (Azure), adaptive customization (Amazon Active Custom Translation (ACT) at $60 per million characters), and review by native speakers.

## Implementation Reality

### Timeline
A proof of concept takes days. Integration takes weeks. Quality validation with native speakers takes longer than the integration, and production rollout needs monitoring and a fallback.

### Skills
A backend engineer for the integration, a native speaker per target language for validation, and someone to own terminology.

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Shipping without native-speaker review.**
2. **Translating sentence by sentence,** which loses context.
3. **Ignoring free-allowance limits and expiry.** Amazon's free tier lasts 12 months.
4. **Assuming equal quality across language pairs.** Test the pairs you ship.
5. **Skipping register control for Japanese business text.** Casual output damages relationships; use a service with a formality setting and have a native reader check it.
6. **Forgetting terminology.** Glossaries keep product names stable.

### First 90 Days
Weeks 1-2: proof of concept with real text. Weeks 3-6: glossary, review workflow, monitoring. Weeks 7-12: production rollout and quality tracking with native-speaker sampling.

## Summary
Machine translation APIs make large-scale CJK translation practical. Choose by required features (register control, documents, customization), ecosystem fit, volume and price, and validate with native speakers. Confirm current prices before costing: DeepL's plans, Azure's standard rate and Google's rates were unconfirmed on 2026-11-05.

# Amazon Translate

## Overview
Amazon Translate is AWS's translation service, integrated with S3, Lambda, Comprehend and Transcribe. It offers real-time and batch translation, custom terminology, Active Custom Translation (ACT), formality, brevity and profanity masking.

## CJK language support
Chinese (Simplified, code zh), Chinese (Traditional, zh-TW), Japanese and Korean are listed in a table of roughly 75 languages (AWS language page, 2026-11-05).

## Formality
The setting applies to 11 target languages: Dutch, French, French (Canada), German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Portugal), Spanish and Spanish (Mexico). Chinese is not listed (AWS formality documentation, 2026-11-05).

## Pricing (AWS pricing page, 2026-11-05)
- Standard text translation: $15 per million characters; free tier of 2M characters a month for 12 months
- Batch document translation: $15 per million characters
- Real-time document translation: text and HTML $15 per million; Docx $30 per million
- Active Custom Translation: $60 per million characters; free allowance of 500K characters a month for 2 months
- Parallel data storage: 200 GB free per account, then $0.023 per GB per month
- Above 1 billion characters a month, AWS asks customers to contact sales

## Documents
Batch translation reads from S3. Real-time document translation covers text, HTML and Docx, to or from English only.

## SDKs
Translate is part of boto3 (1.43.125 on PyPI) and @aws-sdk/client-translate (3.1163.0 on npm), both released 2026-11-05.

## Fit
- Good for: AWS stacks, Japanese and Korean register control, adaptive customization from parallel data
- Weaker for: CJK-to-CJK real-time documents, workloads sensitive to ACT's per-character rate

# Azure Translator

## Overview
Azure Translator is Microsoft's translation service, now under Microsoft Foundry branding. Text API version 2026-06-06 is generally available and adds LLM-based translation next to NMT (Microsoft Foundry blog, 2026-06-02).

## CJK language support
Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean are supported, with transliteration and dictionary scopes for selected languages (Translator languages method, page dated 2026-06-02).

## Pricing
- Free tier (F0): 2 million characters a month for standard translation and custom translation training (Azure pricing page, 2026-11-05).
- Standard pay-as-you-go rate: the pricing page showed no figure on 2026-11-05. The January 2026 list price was $10 per million characters, unconfirmed since.
- Custom Translator: training is capped at $300 per run, hosting is a flat monthly fee per model per region, and the volume tier lists $40 per million custom translation characters and $10 per million training characters.
- Documents: text documents bill per character; image files bill per thousand images of up to 500 characters each.
- LLM translation: billed by input and output tokens, and requires a Microsoft Foundry resource.

## Features
- NMT and, since API 2026-06-06, LLM translation with a choice of model deployment, tone (formal, informal, neutral) and gender controls, plus adaptive custom translation from reference examples.
- Document translation (batch up to 40 MB per document and 1,000 files; synchronous up to 10 MB), glossaries, transliteration, dictionary lookup, custom models.
- Whether tone control changes Japanese politeness level is unconfirmed.

## Limits (Microsoft service limits page dated 2026-08-11)
50,000 characters per translate request; F0 2 million characters per hour, S1 40 million, S3 120 million; custom models 3,600 characters per second per model.

## SDKs
azure-ai-translation-text 2.0.0 on PyPI (2026-05-29) and @azure-rest/ai-translation-text 2.0.0 on npm (2026-05-28). Version 2.0.0 is a major release, so 1.x code needs a migration check.

## Fit
- Good for: Azure stacks, high volume, a large free tier, LLM and NMT behind one service
- Weaker for: Japanese register control on the NMT path

# DeepL API

## Overview
DeepL is a German company known for translation quality, strongest on European languages. It sells a REST API with official SDKs for several languages, and has added a next-generation LLM-based model for some CJK pairs.

## CJK language support
- Chinese: Simplified and Traditional
- Japanese and Korean
- DeepL describes a next-generation model for Japanese and Simplified Chinese, announced in 2025, and reports about 1.7x better results than its previous model for English to Japanese and English to Simplified Chinese in its own blind tests (vendor: DeepL 2025 press release).
- DeepL's language table renders from a keyed endpoint, so per-language formality and glossary coverage follows earlier vendor documentation.

## Pricing
- January 2026 list prices, unconfirmed since: API Free 500K characters a month; API Pro $5.49 a month plus $25 per million characters.
- Sources from mid-2026 describe Free and Pro as closed to new signups, replaced by a one-time 1M-character developer plan and a Growth plan with a monthly fee. DeepL's own pricing pages showed no API tables on 2026-11-05, so the current plan structure is unconfirmed. Check deepl.com/pro-api before quoting a price.
- Usage counts Unicode code points of source text, for text and documents alike; document formats (Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, ODT) bill a minimum of 50,000 characters each, while text-like formats (text, HTML, JSON) bill their exact count (developers.deepl.com usage limits, 2026-11-05).

## Features
Text translation, document translation (Word, PowerPoint, PDF, HTML, text and more), glossaries, formal and informal register for supported targets including Japanese, and XML and HTML tag handling. DeepL's API documentation describes no custom-model training; customization goes through glossaries, style rules and translation memories.

## Data handling
DeepL's developer documentation says texts sent through the Pro API are not stored persistently and not used to train models. Free-tier terms allow DeepL to reuse submitted text, per a third-party summary of the terms.

## SDKs
deepl 1.33.0 on PyPI and deepl-node 1.29.0 on npm, both released 2026-11-02. DeepL marks its legacy v2 languages endpoint deprecated in favor of v3.

## Fit
- Good for: European-to-CJK work, document workflows, Japanese register control
- Weaker for: pure CJK-to-CJK volume, custom model training, teams that need a cloud provider's ecosystem

# Google Cloud Translation

## Overview
Google Cloud Translation is the longest-running of the four services. It offers neural machine translation (NMT), a Translation LLM, adaptive translation, AutoML custom models, batch and document translation, glossaries and transliteration.

## CJK language support
- NMT: zh-CN (Simplified), zh-TW (Traditional), ja, ko; Cantonese (yue) is also listed.
- Translation LLM: zh, zh-CN, zh-Hans, zh-Hant, zh-TW, zh-HK, ja, ko.
- AutoML custom models: Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean paired with English.
- Transliteration is listed for Japanese.

Source: docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/languages, 2026-11-05.

## Pricing
The Google pricing page showed no figures when fetched on 2026-11-05. The figures below come from snippets of that page and January 2026 notes, and are unconfirmed.

- Translation LLM: $10 per million input characters and $10 per million output characters
- Adaptive translation: $25 per million characters each way; cached page versions show discounted tiers tied to savings plans
- AutoML custom models: tiered, at a higher rate than NMT
- Document translation: billed per page

## Features
Glossaries, batch translation from Cloud Storage, document translation, language detection, model choice per request (NMT, LLM, adaptive, custom), REST and gRPC. No formality control was found in Google's documentation.

## SDKs
google-cloud-translate 3.28.0 on PyPI (2026-10-01) and @google-cloud/translate 10.3.0 on npm (2026-10-12).

## Fit
- Good for: Google Cloud stacks, broad general CJK quality, NMT and LLM modes in one service

# Amazon Translate: Feature Detail

## API design
AWS SDK access with IAM authentication, real-time and asynchronous batch modes, and S3 integration. SDKs: boto3 1.43.125 (PyPI) and @aws-sdk/client-translate 3.1163.0 (npm), both released 2026-11-05.

## Languages
Roughly 75 languages including Chinese (Simplified, zh), Chinese (Traditional, zh-TW), Japanese and Korean (AWS language page, 2026-11-05).

## Features
- Formality for 11 target languages: Dutch, French, French (Canada), German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Portugal), Spanish and Spanish (Mexico). Chinese is not among them. Japanese uses polite (teineigo) or casual (kudaketa) forms.
- Brevity and profanity masking for the languages listed on their feature pages.
- Custom terminology for fixed term pairs.
- Active Custom Translation (ACT): adapts output from parallel data supplied at request time, with no model training or hosting step, at $60 per million characters, with 500K characters a month free for 2 months.
- Batch document translation from S3 and real-time document translation for text, HTML and Docx, to or from English only.

## Pricing (AWS, 2026-11-05)
Standard text and batch documents $15 per million characters; real-time documents $15 per million for text and HTML and $30 per million for Docx; ACT $60 per million. Free tier 2M characters a month for 12 months from the first request. Parallel data storage free to 200 GB, then $0.023 per GB per month. Volumes above 1 billion characters a month are priced through AWS sales.

## Gaps
No LLM-based translation mode was found; real-time documents are English-paired; no Chinese formality; ACT requires parallel data.

# Azure Translator: Feature Detail

## API design
Text translation API version 2026-06-06 is generally available; document translation is a separate batch and synchronous service. Authentication uses keys or Microsoft Entra ID. SDKs: azure-ai-translation-text 2.0.0 (PyPI, 2026-05-29), @azure-rest/ai-translation-text 2.0.0 (npm, 2026-05-28) and azure-ai-translation-document 2.0.0 (PyPI, 2026-07-29). The 2.0.0 releases are major versions.

## Languages
Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean, with transliteration for Japanese and a dictionary scope for selected pairs (languages method, page dated 2026-06-02).

## Features
- NMT and LLM-based translation. The LLM path takes a model deployment (a GPT deployment such as GPT-5.1 per Microsoft documentation), tone (formal, informal, neutral) and gender controls, and requires a Foundry resource. Adaptive custom translation uses reference examples or dataset IDs and may avoid retraining; output quality varies (Microsoft Foundry blog, 2026-06-02).
- Custom Translator for trained models; glossaries; batch document translation up to 40 MB per document, 1,000 files and 250 MB per batch, 10 target languages per batch; synchronous documents up to 10 MB.
- Transliteration and dictionary lookup.
- Tone control for Japanese politeness is unconfirmed.

## Limits (page dated 2026-08-11)
50,000 characters per request; F0 2M characters per hour, S1 and S2 40M, S3 120M, S4 200M; custom models 3,600 characters per second per model; maximum latency 15 seconds for standard models and 120 seconds for custom models.

## Pricing
F0 provides 2M characters a month (2026-11-05). The standard rate was not shown that day; the January 2026 list price was $10 per million characters, unconfirmed since. LLM translation bills by tokens. Custom training is capped at $300 per run and hosting is a flat monthly fee per model.

## Gaps
Formality control on the NMT path; fewer public CJK quality comparisons; the LLM path requires a Foundry resource.

# DeepL: Feature Detail

## API design
REST API with API-key authentication and official SDKs (deepl 1.33.0 on PyPI and deepl-node 1.29.0 on npm, released 2026-11-02). Free and paid plans use separate hosts. Request size is limited to 128 KiB (developers.deepl.com, 2026-11-05).

## Languages
Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean are supported. A language table with per-language feature flags is available only through a keyed endpoint, so per-language details follow earlier vendor documentation: formal and informal register for Japanese and several European languages, and glossary support for many pairs. No formality setting is documented for Chinese or Korean.

## Features
- Text and document translation (Word, PowerPoint, PDF, HTML, text, Excel, XML, subtitles and more). Paid plans accept documents up to 100 MB and 5M characters for Word, PowerPoint and PDF; free plans 10 MB and 500K characters. Document formats (Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, ODT) bill at least 50,000 characters each; text-like formats (text, HTML, JSON) bill their exact count.
- Glossaries, style rules and translation memories; tag handling for markup.
- A next-generation LLM-based model for some pairs, including Japanese and Simplified Chinese; DeepL reports about 1.7x better results than its previous model for English to Japanese and English to Simplified Chinese in its own blind tests (vendor: DeepL 2025 press release).
- Voice translation as a separate product.
- DeepL's API documentation describes no custom-model training.

## Pricing
January 2026 list prices, unconfirmed since: Free 500K characters a month; Pro $5.49 a month plus $25 per million characters. Sources from mid-2026 report a changed plan structure (a one-time developer plan and a Growth plan); the current plans are unconfirmed.

## Data handling
Pro API texts are not stored persistently or used to train models (DeepL developer documentation). Free-tier terms allow reuse (third-party summary).

## Gaps
No published rate limits were found; no custom-model training described in its API documentation; no batch text mode comparable to cloud object-store batch jobs; no cloud-provider ecosystem integration.

# Google Cloud Translation: Feature Detail

## API design
Two API generations: a basic text API and an advanced API with glossaries, batch jobs, documents and model selection, over REST and gRPC. Authentication uses API keys or service accounts. SDKs: google-cloud-translate 3.28.0 (2026-10-01) and @google-cloud/translate 10.3.0 (2026-10-12).

## Languages
NMT covers zh-CN, zh-TW, ja, ko and Cantonese. The Translation LLM table lists zh, zh-CN, zh-Hans, zh-Hant, zh-TW, zh-HK, ja and ko, and the same languages work for adaptive translation. AutoML custom models cover Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean paired with English. Japanese transliteration is listed (docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/languages, 2026-11-05).

## Features
- NMT, Translation LLM and adaptive translation (examples supplied at request time), AutoML custom models trained on parallel data.
- Glossaries, batch translation from Cloud Storage, document translation, language detection.
- No formality control was found in Google's documentation.

# S4 Strategic Recommendation: Long-Term Viability

## Executive Summary: 3-5 Year Outlook

All four providers are strategically viable for CJK translation with varying risk profiles:

| Provider | Viability | Strategic Risk | Best For (Long-Term) |
|----------|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Google** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Excellent | Low | Enterprise, GCP-native, proven track record |
| **Azure** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Excellent | Low | Azure-native, high-volume, LLM and NMT in one service |
| **Amazon** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Excellent | Low | AWS-native, Japanese and Korean register control, adaptive customization (ACT) |
| **DeepL** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Good | Medium | Quality-focused, European+CJK, independent |

**Key Insight:** Cloud platform providers (Google/Azure/Amazon) have the lowest strategic risk because translation is a core cloud AI service and ecosystem coupling works in your favor through continuing investment. These ratings are judgments, not measurements.

## Provider Assessment

### Google Cloud Translation
- Core Google Cloud AI service with a long translation lineage
- Offers NMT, Translation LLM, adaptive translation and AutoML in one product (docs.cloud.google.com, 2026-11-05)
- Official SDKs current: 3.28.0 on PyPI (2026-10-01), 10.3.0 on npm (2026-10-12)
- Gap: no formality control found in the documentation
- Lock-in: medium to high through Cloud Storage and IAM; REST API is portable

### Azure Translator
- Core Azure AI service, now branded under Microsoft Foundry
- API 2026-06-06 is generally available with LLM translation, tone and gender controls and adaptive custom translation, with NMT kept as a model choice (Microsoft Foundry blog, 2026-06-02)
- Python and JavaScript text SDKs reached 2.0.0 on 2026-05-29 and 2026-05-28, a major version that needs a migration check for 1.x code
- Gap: formality on the NMT path; tone behavior for Japanese unconfirmed
- Lock-in: medium to high through Blob Storage and Entra ID

### Amazon Translate
- Core AWS AI service, with boto3 and the AWS JavaScript client released 2026-11-05
- Formality for 11 target languages including Japanese and Korean; ACT customization at $60 per million characters; batch and real-time documents, with real-time documents English-paired (AWS, 2026-11-05)
- Gap: no LLM translation mode found; no Chinese formality
- Lock-in: medium to high through S3, Lambda and IAM; ACT parallel data is portable but workflow-dependent

### DeepL
- Independent company focused on translation quality; next-generation LLM-based model for some CJK pairs
- Official SDKs current: deepl 1.33.0 and deepl-node 1.29.0 (2026-11-02); v2 languages endpoint deprecated in favor of v3
- Plan structure unconfirmed: sources from mid-2026 report Free and Pro closed to new signups in favor of new plans
- Risks: independence means acquisition or pricing change is possible; no acquisition news was found in the sources read
- Lock-in: low to medium; a standalone REST API is easiest to move

## Technology Trends
1. **LLM modes beside NMT.** Google and Azure ship them; DeepL describes a next-generation model. Quality differences among services may narrow, which would make price, limits and ecosystem more important.
2. **Register control converging.** Amazon and DeepL document it; Azure added tone on its LLM path in 2026. Google was not found to offer it.
3. **Documents as a standard feature.** All four translate documents; Amazon's real-time path pairs with English only.
4. **Customization without training.** Amazon ACT, Google adaptive translation and Azure adaptive custom translation all adapt output from examples at request time.

## General-Purpose LLM APIs as a Category Member
Sending text to a chat-model API is a working alternative to a dedicated translation service. It is priced per token rather than per character, gives prompt-level control over register and terminology, and has no fixed glossary format. No primary-source benchmark of its CJK quality, cost at volume or run-to-run consistency was found. Points to check: determinism, data terms, per-token cost on long documents. Dedicated services keep fixed per-character pricing, document handling, glossaries and published limits.

## SDK Maintenance
Registry probe 2026-11-05: official SDKs for all four vendors were released within the last six months. Unofficial wrappers are weaker: googletrans last released 2025-01-01 and deep-translator 2023-06-28. A wrapper that scrapes a consumer endpoint carries service-terms and breakage risk that a keyed API does not.

## Strategy by Situation
- **Already on a cloud:** use that cloud's service as the primary; it has the lowest integration cost.
- **Japanese or Korean register control is central:** Amazon or DeepL; test Azure's LLM tone.
- **Mixed European and CJK content:** DeepL as the primary, with a cloud service as backup.
- **Hedge:** keep translation behind an internal interface so the vendor, or an LLM route, can change without touching callers.

## Monitoring Triggers
DeepL plan announcements; Google pricing page changes; Azure LLM model deployment changes; AWS additions of LLM or Chinese formality; deprecation notices for API versions in use.
